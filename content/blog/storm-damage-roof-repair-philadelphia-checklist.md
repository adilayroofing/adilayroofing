## Step 1: Safety First — From the Ground

Before anything else:

- **Stay off the roof.** Storm-damaged decking can be structurally compromised, and wet surfaces are treacherous even for professionals with harnesses.
- **Check for downed lines.** If a tree or limb came down anywhere near a service line, treat every wire as live and call PECO before anyone works in the area.
- **If a tree hit the house, get out and get it assessed.** A limb through the roof deck can crack rafters. Structural assessment comes before everything else on this list.
- **Inside, contain the water.** Buckets under drips, plastic over furniture. If ceiling paint is bulging with trapped water, poke a small hole with a screwdriver and drain it into a bucket — a controlled drip beats a collapsed ceiling.

## Step 2: Document Everything Before Anything Is Touched

This is the step people skip, and it's the one the insurance process runs on.

- **Exterior photos from the ground**: every side of the house, the roof line, visible missing shingles, debris, the tree limb where it fell.
- **Interior photos**: every water stain, drip, and wet spot — with a timestamp.
- **Wide shots and close-ups both.** Wide shots prove context (this is my house, this is the storm date); close-ups prove damage.
- **Note the storm date and time.** Your claim will be matched against weather records; make it easy.

Ten minutes with a phone camera now is worth thousands of dollars later. When our crew arrives, we add roof-level documentation — but your ground-level "before" photos are evidence nobody can argue with.

## Step 3: Stop the Bleeding — Tarp If the Envelope Is Open

If water can reach the interior, get the roof tarped the same day. Your policy's mitigation clause actually requires you to prevent further damage, and interior damage that spreads while a roof sits open for a week is the easiest thing for a carrier to push back on.

## Step 4: Get a Licensed Local Roofer's Assessment — Before You File

Not every storm-damaged roof is an insurance claim. The order matters:

1. **A roofer inspects and documents** — photos, measurements, and a written scope of what the storm actually did.
2. **You compare the honest repair number against your deductible.** A $700 shingle repair with a $1,000 deductible is not a claim; it's a repair. Filing it costs you claim history and gains you nothing.
3. **If it is claim-worthy, you file with documentation in hand** — a professional scope of work and photo set, instead of "some shingles blew off, I think."

## Step 5: Know the Damage Types (and What Each Means)

**Wind-lifted and missing shingles.** The most common Philadelphia storm damage. Missing tabs are obvious; *creased* tabs are not — a shingle that lifted in the wind and laid back down has a broken seal and a crease line, and it will leak or blow off in the next event. An inspection catches creasing that ground-level photos miss.

**Tree and limb impact.** Punctures range from a few broken shingles to a hole in the deck. Even a "small" limb strike deserves a deck check — impact cracks travel.

**Hail bruising.** Random dark spots where granules were knocked off, dented gutters and flashing. Subtle, commonly missed, and commonly disputed by carriers — documentation quality decides these claims.

**Flashing and penetration damage.** Wind-driven rain exploits chimney flashing, vent boots, and valley metal. If your ceiling stain appeared after wind-driven rain but the shingle field looks intact, flashing is the usual suspect — see our guide to [chimney flashing done right](/blog/chimney-flashing-installed-correctly-philadelphia).

**Flat-roof membrane peel-back.** Rowhome EPDM and modified bitumen roofs fail at edges and terminations in high wind. A peeled corner is urgent — the next gust turns it into a peeled roof.

## Step 6: Avoid the Storm Chasers

After every major Philadelphia-area storm, out-of-town crews canvas damaged neighborhoods. The playbook: knock on doors, promise a "free roof through insurance," pressure you to sign an assignment-of-benefits agreement, collect deposits, do fast work or none at all, and be gone before the first callback.

Protect yourself with three questions:

1. **"What's your PA contractor registration number?"** Verify it on the state's registry. (Ours is PA184779.)
2. **"What's your local address and how long have you worked in Philadelphia?"** Storm chasers have neither.
3. **"Can I have the estimate in writing, with no signature today?"** Legitimate contractors expect you to compare quotes. Pressure to sign on the spot is itself the red flag. Our guide to [roofing estimate red flags](/blog/roofing-estimate-red-flags-philadelphia) covers the full list.
