# Roof Insurance Claims in Philadelphia: What the Adjuster Looks For

Most homeowners go through a roof insurance claim exactly once or twice in their lives. The adjuster who shows up at your house has done hundreds. That information gap — not bad faith — is why so many Philadelphia homeowners walk away from claims feeling like they negotiated blind.

This guide closes the gap. It covers what the adjuster is actually evaluating on your roof, the claim timeline step by step, why claims get denied, how supplements recover missed costs, and where a contractor fits in the process — including what we do on the roofs we handle.

## The One Question Every Adjuster Is Answering

Homeowner's insurance covers **sudden, accidental damage** — wind, hail, falling trees, fire. It does not cover **wear, age, or maintenance failures**. Every minute the adjuster spends on your roof is spent sorting your damage into one of those two buckets.

**Evidence for a covered event:**

- Missing or creased shingles concentrated on the slopes that faced the storm's wind
- Shingle tabs with broken adhesive seals and fresh crease lines
- Hail bruises — random circular granule loss with soft spots — plus matching dents on gutters, downspouts, and vent caps
- Torn or peeled flat-roof membrane at edges and corners
- Impact punctures from limbs, with fresh wood/debris evidence

**Evidence for the age bucket (working against the claim):**

- Uniform granule loss across all slopes, granules filling the gutters
- Curling, cupping, or brittle shingles
- Previous patch repairs and sealant blobs
- Multiple shingle layers (a prior roof-over)
- Rusted flashing, aged sealant, generally end-of-life surfaces

Here's the honest part most contractors won't say: **if your roof is genuinely at end-of-life, a storm claim is not a replacement plan.** Carriers pay to restore storm damage, not to retire old roofs. An inspection before filing tells you which situation you have — and filing a doomed claim costs you claim history for nothing.

## The Claim Timeline, Step by Step

1. **Storm hits. You mitigate and document.** Tarp if water is entering (your policy requires preventing further damage), and photograph everything first. Our [after-storm checklist](/blog/storm-damage-roof-repair-philadelphia-checklist) covers this stage in detail.
2. **A licensed roofer inspects — before you file.** You learn whether the damage clears your deductible and get a written scope with photos. Free at [Adilay Roofing](/get-quote).
3. **You file promptly.** Policies require timely notice. Give the storm date, describe the damage factually, and submit your photo documentation.
4. **The carrier assigns an adjuster; the inspection gets scheduled.** Typically one to three weeks out — longer after a major regional storm.
5. **The adjuster inspection.** Twenty to forty minutes on and around the roof. This visit produces the scope and dollar figure everything else builds on. **Have your contractor there** (more below).
6. **The estimate and first payment arrive.** Carriers typically pay actual cash value first, holding back recoverable depreciation until work completes. The check plus depreciation minus your deductible should fund the actual repair.
7. **Work happens; supplements if needed.** If tear-off reveals hidden deck damage or code items the scope missed, the contractor documents and submits a supplement — a normal part of the process.
8. **Completion documentation; depreciation released.** Final invoice and photos go to the carrier; the held-back depreciation is paid out.

## Why the Adjuster Meeting Matters More Than Anything Else

The single highest-leverage move in the entire process costs nothing: **have your roofer on the roof with the adjuster.**

The adjuster walks the roof once. What makes it into their notes becomes the claim; what doesn't, doesn't. A contractor present in that window:

- Confirms measurements and scope match what the repair will actually require, so the first estimate is close to right and the supplement process stays small

## Denials and Disputes: A First Offer, Not Always a Final Answer

Claims get denied or underpaid for the reasons listed in the FAQ above — and the wear-vs-storm judgment call is by far the most common. When you believe the call is wrong:

1. **Request the denial in writing** with the specific policy language relied on.
2. **Get a documented second inspection** — close-up photos, test-square hail counts, weather-data correlation for the storm date.
3. **Request a re-inspection with your contractor present.** Many "denials" soften when the evidence is walked through shingle by shingle.
4. **Escalate if warranted**: every carrier has an internal appeal path, and the Pennsylvania Insurance Department takes consumer complaints. For large disputed losses, a licensed public adjuster (who represents you, for a percentage) can make sense — that's a different role from the carrier's adjuster.

What we bring to a dispute is evidence quality: adjusters respond to documentation, not frustration.

## Three Things to Never Do

1. **Don't sign an assignment of benefits at the door.** Post-storm canvassers push AOB agreements that hand your claim to them. You lose control of your own claim; they gain a blank check. Expert help doesn't require surrendering your benefits — see our [estimate red flags guide](/blog/roofing-estimate-red-flags-philadelphia).
2. **Don't leave the roof open "for the adjuster to see."** Photos preserve evidence. An open roof grows interior damage the carrier can then refuse as unmitigated.
3. **Don't inflate, and don't let anyone inflate for you.** "Free roof" promises from contractors who plan to game the claim put your name on the fraud. The process works fine on real damage, honestly documented.
