## What You're Actually Buying: The Anatomy of the Price

A flat roof replacement isn't just membrane. On a typical rowhome job the money goes to:

- **Tear-off and disposal.** Stripping the old roof (or roofs — many Philly rowhomes carry two generations of material) and hauling it. More layers, more labor, more disposal weight.
- **Deck assessment and repair.** The plywood or plank deck under your membrane is the part nobody can see until tear-off. Small rot repairs are routine; widespread soft decking is the biggest legitimate mid-job addition, which is why we probe for moisture *before* quoting and price deck repair per-square in the estimate so surprises are priced before they're found.
- **The membrane system.** Not just the sheet: adhesives or fasteners, insulation or cover board where specified, and the seam work that determines whether the roof lasts 12 years or 28.
- **The details — where flat roofs actually live or die.** Parapet wall terminations, tie-ins to your neighbors' roofs (rowhome roofs are a shared drainage ecosystem), drains and scuppers, edge metal, and every pipe and vent penetration. Detail work is the difference between contractors, and it's where cheap quotes cut.
- **Permit.** Full replacement in Philadelphia generally requires a building permit; we pull it as part of the job ([permit guide here](/blog/roof-permit-philadelphia)).

## Choosing the Membrane (the Short Version)

The full comparison lives in our [EPDM vs TPO vs modified bitumen guide](/blog/epdm-vs-tpo-vs-modified-bitumen-philadelphia); the cost-focused summary:

- **EPDM rubber ($400–$900/square)** — the Philadelphia default for good reason: 25–30 year track record in our freeze-thaw climate, the simplest seam repairs decades from now, and the friendliest price. Our usual recommendation for rowhome roofs.
- **Modified bitumen ($400–$900/square)** — comparable price, best under foot traffic; the right call if your roof doubles as deck access.
- **TPO ($600–$1,200/square)** — the premium white option; reflects summer heat (real comfort difference for third-floor bedrooms), heat-welded seams, higher labor cost.

Beware any quote that just says "rubber roof" — membrane thickness matters. 60-mil EPDM and a bargain thin sheet are different products with different lifespans at different prices, and a vague quote hides that difference.

## Tear-Off vs. Overlay: The 20–30% Question

An overlay — new membrane over the old roof — legitimately saves 20–30% *when the conditions are right*: one existing layer, dry insulation, sound deck. Philadelphia code generally prohibits a third layer, so a previously overlaid roof must tear off.

The trap is overlaying trapped moisture. Covering a wet roof seals water against your deck, and the new membrane's warranty won't save the rotting wood under it. We take moisture readings on every estimate; the honest recommendation comes from the probe, not the price sheet. ## What Makes Your Roof Cost More (or Less) Than Your Neighbor's

Same block, same footprint, different price — here's why:

- **Access.** A three-story with front-only ladder access stages slower than a two-story with a rear walkout. Material hoisting time is real labor.
- **Layer count.** One tear-off layer vs two changes a day of work.
- **Deck condition.** The roof that leaked for three years before replacement carries deck repairs the well-maintained roof doesn't.
- **Detail density.** Skylights, multiple stacks, HVAC penetrations, deteriorated parapet caps — each is detail work done right or a future leak.
- **Drainage corrections.** If your old roof ponded, replacement is the moment to fix slope with tapered insulation — worth doing, priced in the estimate, and covered in our [ponding water guide](/blog/ponding-water-flat-roof-philadelphia).

## Replacement vs. Repair: A Sanity Check

Not every failing flat roof needs replacement. A single seam split or one bad drain flashing on an otherwise sound membrane is a [repair](/services/flat-roof-repair), often a few hundred dollars. The replacement conversation starts when leaks recur in different spots, seams are failing generally, the membrane is brittle or blistered across the field, or moisture readings show water in the system. We tell you which side of the line your roof is on — with photos — because a $600 repair that buys five years is sometimes the right answer, and we'd rather be the company you call five years later.
