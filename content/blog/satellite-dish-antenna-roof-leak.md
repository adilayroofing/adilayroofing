# Old Satellite Dishes and Antennas: The Hidden Leak on Philadelphia Roofs

Look across the rooftops of almost any Philadelphia block and you'll see them: gray satellite dishes pointed at the sky, old TV antennas, rusted brackets with nothing on them. Most stopped working years ago when the house switched to cable or streaming. But every one of them is still bolted through somebody's roof.

Removing old satellite dishes and antennas is one of the most frequent first steps on our roof scopes. Here's why they cause leaks and how they should come off.

## Why Dishes and Antennas Leak

**They're bolted through the roof.** A typical dish mount uses four or more lag bolts through the roofing into the deck. Each is a hole.

**The seal is just caulk.** Installers usually squirt sealant under and over the mount. Sealant has a limited life in sun and freeze-thaw, and once it cracks, water runs down the bolts into the wood.

**On flat roofs they sit right on the membrane.** A mount on a flat rowhome roof often rests directly on the membrane, where it holds water, rubs as it moves in the wind, and wears through.

**Cables add more holes.** The coax cable usually runs across the roof and down the wall through yet another hole or clip.

**Nobody looks at them.** Once a dish is abandoned, no one checks it. The sealant fails slowly, and the wood under it rots slowly.

## Signs a Mount Is Leaking

- A ceiling stain on the top floor that lines up with where the dish or antenna is
- Rust streaks running down from the mount
- A soft spot on a flat roof around the mount
- Cracked or missing sealant around the bolts
- A loose mount that moves when pushed

## How Old Mounts Should Come Off

Just unbolting a dish and walking away leaves a set of open holes. Smearing caulk over them is a short-term patch. The right way:

1. **Remove the dish, antenna and mount completely**, including brackets and cable clips.
2. **Inspect the wood under the holes.** Long-term leaks at mounts often rot the plywood. Soft wood gets replaced.
3. **Seal the holes properly.** On a flat roof, the area gets new membrane torched over it — or, on a full replacement, the new roof simply covers a clean deck. On a shingle roof, damaged shingles are replaced and the holes sealed under them.
4. **Haul the old equipment away.**

The best time to do this is when the roof is being repaired or replaced, which is why "remove old satellite dish and antenna from main roof" shows up as line one on so many of our scopes. It costs very little extra when the crew is already there, and it removes a leak source before the new roof goes on.

## If You Still Use a Dish

If you need a dish, the best place for it is off the roof surface: on a wall, on a chimney mount, or on a pole. If it has to go on the roof, ask for a non-penetrating mount or have a roofer flash the mount properly. And if you're getting a new roof, take the dish down first and have it reinstalled correctly after — never roofed around.

## The Bottom Line

An abandoned satellite dish isn't harmless decoration. It's a set of holes in your roof held shut by sealant that's past its life. If you're not using it, have it removed and the roof sealed — ideally the next time any roof work is done.
