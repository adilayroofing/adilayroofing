# What Is Roof Coping (Capping) — and Why Philadelphia Rowhomes Need It

Ask most homeowners what their roof is made of and they'll describe the surface: rubber, tar, shingles. Very few mention the edges. But on a Philadelphia rowhome, the edges are where the trouble starts. And the most important piece at the edge is something most people have never heard named: the **coping**, or as most Philly roofers call it, the **capping**.

Coping is one of the most common lines on the roof scopes we write — up there with plywood and membrane. Here's what it is, how it fails, and why it belongs in every flat roof job.

## What Coping Is

Coping is a metal cover over the top of a wall or roof edge. On Philadelphia rowhomes it's usually bent aluminum, white or black, running along:

- The **front and back edges** of the roof
- The top of **parapet walls** — the short brick walls that stick up above the roof line
- The **edges of porch, bay window and back-building roofs**
- Sometimes the **side edges** on end-of-row homes

Its job is simple: keep water out of the top of the wall. Brick and mortar soak up water. If rain can get into the top of a parapet, it runs down inside the wall and comes out wherever it finds a path — often a stain on the top-floor wall or ceiling that seems unrelated to the roof.

## How Coping Works With Flashing

On a rowhome roof edge there are two layers of protection:

1. **The membrane and flashing.** The roof membrane runs up and over the edge or up the face of the parapet, and aluminum flashing set in flashing cement seals it.
2. **The coping.** Bent aluminum caps the top of the wall or edge, covering the top of the flashing so water can't get behind it.

Water hits the coping, runs off onto the roof or over the edge, and never touches the brick or the top of the membrane. When either layer fails, water gets in.

## How Coping Fails

- **It comes loose.** Fasteners back out, wind lifts the edges, and the coping shifts.
- **The joints open.** Coping comes in lengths. Where pieces meet, gaps open as the metal expands and contracts.
- **The cement underneath dries out.** Flashing cement eventually cracks. Once it does, water can track under the coping.
- **It was never there.** Many older roofs have no real coping — just membrane wrapped over the edge and painted with roof coating.
- **It was put back on a new roof.** Bent, old coping reinstalled over new membrane is a common shortcut.

## Signs Your Coping Is Failing

From the street, the alley or a back window, look for:

- Coping pieces that are **bent, lifted or missing**
- **Gaps** between sections
- **Rust streaks or stains** running down the brick below the roof edge
- **Peeling paint or stains** on the top-floor walls, especially along the front or back
- **Loose or crumbling mortar** at the top of a parapet
- Water dripping off the edge in the wrong place — behind the gutter instead of into it

Water getting past the coping often shows up as [water behind the gutter](/blog/water-leaking-behind-gutter-philadelphia) or rotted fascia.

## How We Install New Coping

On a new flat roof, or an edge repair, the steps are:

1. **Remove the old coping and flashing** so the edge is exposed.
2. **Inspect and repair** the wood edge or brick underneath. Soft wood is replaced; cracked brick is sealed.
3. **Run the new membrane** up the parapet or over the edge.
4. **Install new aluminum flashing** along the edge, set in flashing cement.
5. **Install new aluminum coping** over the top of the wall or edge, lapped at the joints and fastened properly.

We use white or black aluminum — whatever suits the house — and heavier-gauge metal where the edge takes a beating.

## Coping Around Windows

The same bent aluminum is used to cover the wood trim around windows. On a lot of Philadelphia rowhome fronts, the wood frames around the windows are the next thing to rot after the roof edge. Covering them in aluminum coping means no more painting and no more rot. It's often done in the same job as a porch roof, bay window roof or new siding on the front wall, since the crew and the metal brake are already there.

## Why It Belongs in Every Flat Roof Job

The membrane gets the attention, but flat roofs rarely fail in the middle. They fail at the edges. If a new roof goes on with old coping put back — or no coping at all — the most vulnerable part of the roof is also the oldest. That's why every flat roof scope we write includes new aluminum flashing and coping around the edges, listed with its own line and linear footage.

## The Bottom Line

Coping is a small, inexpensive part of a roof that does a big job: keeping water out of the walls. Rowhomes have a lot of edges and a lot of parapets, which is why coping matters more here than almost anywhere else.
