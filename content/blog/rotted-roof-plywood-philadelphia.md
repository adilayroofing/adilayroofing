# Rotted Roof Plywood: What Happens When Your Roofer Finds It

On almost every Philadelphia roof we open up, there's a moment where the old roof comes off and the wood underneath tells the real story. Sometimes it's dry and solid. Often, especially on rowhomes that have leaked for a while, there are soft, dark sections near the drain, along a wall, or around an old satellite mount.

Rotted roof plywood is the most common surprise in roofing. It's also where a lot of trust between homeowners and contractors is won or lost. This guide explains why it happens, how a good contractor handles it, and what your estimate should say before any work starts.

## What the Roof Deck Is

Under every roof is a deck: the layer of plywood (or on older homes, wooden boards) that the roofing is attached to. On a flat rowhome roof, the membrane is bonded to it. On a sloped roof, underlayment and shingles are nailed through it. The deck is what you're actually standing on when you walk on a roof.

When the deck is sound, the roof above it has a solid base. When it's rotted, the roofing sags, fasteners pull out, seams stress and the new roof fails early at exactly those spots.

## Why Plywood Rots

Wood rots when it stays wet. On Philadelphia roofs, that almost always traces back to water getting through the roofing for months or years:

- **Leaks at the edges and walls** — failed coping, flashing or wall joints
- **Clogged or rusted drain boxes** that let water back up on flat roofs
- **Old penetrations** — satellite dishes, antennas, abandoned vent pipes
- **Chimneys** with failed flashing
- **Ponding** where a sagging deck holds water, which makes the sag worse
- **Poor ventilation** on sloped roofs, which traps moisture under the deck in winter

Because water travels along the deck before it shows up inside, the rotted area is often larger than the ceiling stain suggests.

## Where We Find It Most

On rowhome flat roofs, rot clusters in predictable places:

- Around the **drain box** at the back of the roof
- Along **parapet walls** and the joint with the neighbor's roof
- At the **front and back edges**, under failed coping
- Around **old satellite and antenna mounts**
- Under **porch and bay window roofs**, especially where they meet the house

On sloped roofs, it's around chimneys, in valleys, along the eaves where ice dams form, and anywhere a skylight has leaked.

## How a Good Contractor Handles It

Rotted decking can't be measured exactly until the old roof is off. That's a fact of roofing, not a trick. What matters is how the contractor handles that uncertainty.

### Before the job: say how it will be handled

The estimate should state plainly that the plywood will be inspected once the old roof is removed, that damaged plywood will be replaced, and how that replacement is charged. Our scopes put it in one line: inspect all wood, replace any damaged plywood, and notify the owner.

### During the job: stop and tell you

When rot is found, you should hear about it before it's replaced — a call, a text or photos. You should know where it is and roughly how much there is. No one should come home to a bigger bill and no explanation.

### Replace it properly

Damaged plywood gets cut out back to solid wood and replaced with new plywood of the right thickness, fastened to the framing. On sloped roofs, new synthetic underlayment and ice &amp; water shield go over it. On flat roofs, the new membrane is installed over the fresh deck.

### Fix the reason it rotted

Replacing wood without fixing the cause just schedules the next rot. If it was the drain box, rebuild the drain box. If it was the edge, install new flashing and coping. If it was a satellite mount, remove it and seal the deck.

## Warning Signs Before the Job

You can often tell there's rot coming before anyone opens the roof:

- **Soft or springy spots** when walking on a flat roof
- **Sagging** or low areas where water collects
- **Long-standing ceiling stains** on the top floor
- **Dark, stained wood** visible from the attic on sloped roofs
- **Nails backing out** or shingles that won't lie flat
- **Mold or a musty smell** in the attic or top floor

None of these tell you exactly how much wood is bad, but they tell you to expect some.

## Red Flags in How Rot Is Handled

- **"We'll just go over it."** New roofing over soft wood is a shortcut that fails early.
- **No mention of plywood on the estimate at all.** Then any rot found becomes a surprise negotiation on the roof.
- **Rot found, replaced and billed without telling you.** You should always hear first.
- **No photos or removed wood to look at.** A contractor who replaced rotted wood can show you what came off.

## The Bottom Line

Finding rotted plywood isn't a sign something went wrong with your roof job. It's a sign the job is being done right — the old roof came off, and the deck is being checked instead of covered. What matters is that it's in the estimate before work starts, you're told when it's found, and the cause gets fixed along with the wood.
