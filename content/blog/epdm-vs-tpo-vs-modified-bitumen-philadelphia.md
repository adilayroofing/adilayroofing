## EPDM (Rubber): The Long-Lasting Workhorse

EPDM (ethylene propylene diene monomer) is a synthetic rubber membrane that's been used on commercial and residential flat roofs since the 1970s. The standard version is jet black, supplied in large sheets (typically 10 to 50 feet wide), and adhered to the deck or to a layer of rigid insulation with either contact adhesive or mechanical fasteners.

### What EPDM Does Well

- **Real-world longevity.** A properly installed EPDM roof on a Philadelphia rowhome with reasonable maintenance regularly lasts 30+ years. We've inspected EPDM roofs installed in the early 1990s that are still in service today.
- **No recoat schedule.** Unlike modified bitumen with its 4-to-7-year silver-coat cycle, EPDM is installed and left alone. No ongoing coating cost.
- **Easy localized repair.** A puncture, a seam separation, or a flashing failure can be patched with EPDM-compatible adhesive and a rubber patch for $400 to $900. Every Philly flat roofer knows how to do it.
- **Flexes well in cold.** Stays flexible at very low temperatures, which matters during Philadelphia's freeze-thaw cycles.

### What EPDM Doesn't Do Well

- **Black color absorbs heat.** A black EPDM roof in full Philadelphia summer sun can reach 160 to 180 degrees Fahrenheit, which transfers heat into the building below and raises cooling costs. If you have a top-floor unit or live in the rear of the rowhome, you'll feel this.
- **Adhesive seams are the weak point.** EPDM is fully cured rubber, so it can't be heat-welded the way TPO can. Seams are bonded with adhesive tape and primer. Older EPDM installs (pre-1995) sometimes used inferior adhesives that failed at 15 to 20 years, but modern systems hold up well.
- **Puncture vulnerability.** EPDM is thin (typically 45 mil or 60 mil) and can be punctured by debris, tools, or careless foot traffic. If you have HVAC techs walking the roof regularly, factor that in.

### EPDM Is the Right Pick When

- You want a long service life with no recoat schedule
- The roof is partly shaded by neighboring buildings, trees, or a parapet wall, so heat absorption isn't a huge issue
- You don't expect heavy foot traffic
- Budget is moderate (EPDM is usually the lowest-cost of the three on most Philly rowhomes)
- You want simple, well-understood maintenance

## TPO: The Energy-Efficient Newcomer

TPO (thermoplastic polyolefin) is a newer single-ply membrane, in widespread use since roughly 2000. It comes in white from the factory (gray and tan also available) and is heat-welded at the seams, creating a chemically-fused bond that's stronger than EPDM's adhesive seams.

### What TPO Does Well

- **Reflectivity.** A white TPO roof reflects 80%+ of incoming UV, keeping the roof surface 40 to 60 degrees cooler than a black EPDM roof on the same day. In a top-floor unit with no shade, the cooling-cost difference is real, often 10 to 20 percent on summer electricity bills.
- **Heat-welded seams.** When done right, a heat-welded TPO seam is the strongest part of the roof. There's no adhesive that can fail with age.
- **Resists punctures slightly better than EPDM** at the same thickness due to the polymer formulation.

### What TPO Doesn't Do Well

- **Quality varies wildly by manufacturer.** Early TPO formulations (2000-2010) had reformulation issues that caused premature failures. Modern formulations from established manufacturers (Carlisle, Firestone, GAF EverGuard, Versico) are solid, but cheap house-brand TPO is risky.
- **Seam-welding is specialized.** A TPO repair requires a heat-welder and a roofer trained on the equipment. Many smaller Philly flat-roof contractors don't do TPO repair, so if the original installer is out of business, finding a qualified repair contractor takes work.
- **More expensive per square foot installed.** Typically $1 to $2 per square foot more than EPDM, $0.50 to $1.50 more than modified bitumen.
- **Long-term aging.** TPO can become brittle after 20 to 25 years of UV exposure, leading to surface cracking. The good brands have improved this, but TPO doesn't have the 40+ year track record EPDM does.

### TPO Is the Right Pick When

- The roof is in full sun and the building has air conditioning that runs hard in summer
- You want a system that reflects heat away rather than absorbs it
- The roof is on a building where you'll be there long enough to recover the higher install cost through energy savings (10+ years)
- You have a contractor with documented TPO experience and a manufacturer-certified installer status

## Modified Bitumen: The Philly Rowhome Standard

Modified bitumen is the workhorse of Philadelphia rowhome flat roofs. It's asphalt-based, reinforced with polymer (either APP or SBS), and installed in two or three plies either by torching (heating the underside as it's rolled out) or with cold-applied adhesives. The top layer is usually coated with reflective aluminum (silver coating) or a granular surface for UV protection.

### What Modified Bitumen Does Well

- **Cheapest to repair.** Patching mod-bit is straightforward: torch a new piece of mod-bit over the failed area or cold-apply with compatible adhesive. Every Philadelphia flat-roof contractor knows the technique. Localized repairs cost $400 to $900.
- **Affordable to install.** Material cost is moderate, labor is well-understood, and most Philly contractors are experienced with it.
- **Multi-ply redundancy.** A 2-ply or 3-ply mod-bit system provides redundancy: a puncture in the top layer doesn't immediately mean a leak because the layers below catch it. Single-ply EPDM and TPO don't have this margin.
- **Handles foot traffic well.** Less puncture-prone than single-ply, easier to walk on without damage.

### What Modified Bitumen Doesn't Do Well

- **Needs ongoing recoats.** The silver top coat is the UV protection. It needs to be recoated every 4 to 7 years to maintain reflectivity and prevent the asphalt-based membrane underneath from drying out. Neglected recoats are the number-one reason mod-bit roofs fail at 15 to 18 years instead of 25 to 30.
- **Torch installation has fire risk.** A torch-down install requires open flame near old wood framing in dense Philadelphia rowhome blocks. Cold-applied mod-bit eliminates the risk but costs more in labor.
- **Lower reflectivity than TPO.** A fresh silver coat reflects roughly 60 to 70 percent of UV; TPO white reflects 80+ percent.
- **Asphalt softens at very high temperatures.** Not usually an issue in Philadelphia, but on extreme summer days, foot traffic should be avoided to prevent indentation.

### Modified Bitumen Is the Right Pick When

- Budget matters and you want to maximize value per dollar
- You want a system that's easy and cheap to repair locally
- You can stay on top of the recoat schedule (we offer a 5-year recoat reminder for our customers)
- The roof has any foot traffic at all (HVAC servicing, satellite, etc.)
- You want a system every Philly roofer can service if something happens to the original installer

## The Philly-Specific Factors

A few things matter more on Philadelphia rowhomes than on the average national flat-roof guide.

**Parapet walls.** Most Philadelphia rowhome flat roofs are framed by a parapet wall (the short brick wall at the front, sometimes also at the rear and sides). Membrane termination at the parapet is the single most common failure point on Philly rowhome roofs. All three membrane types can be terminated correctly, but the detail must be right: through-wall flashing, proper counter flashing, and drainage that doesn't pond against the wall. We covered this failure mode in our [flat roof leaks every winter guide](/blog/flat-roof-leaks-every-winter-philadelphia).

**Narrow alley access.** South Philly, Fishtown, and Kensington rowhome roofs often have to be accessed by carrying materials through the house or up the back alley. EPDM and TPO sheets are large and awkward; mod-bit rolls are heavy. Material handling adds $500 to $1,500 on tight-access jobs regardless of which membrane.

**Old wood decking.** Many pre-1950 rowhomes have wood plank decking (skip sheathing or solid 1x6 boards) rather than modern plywood. EPDM and TPO require a smooth substrate, which usually means installing 1/2" plywood or 1/4" cover board over the existing planks. Mod-bit can sometimes be installed directly over sound plank decking with a base sheet. The substrate decision affects total cost.

**Historic district considerations.** Mount Airy, Chestnut Hill, and parts of Germantown have historic district review for visible exterior work. A flat roof that isn't visible from the public right-of-way usually doesn't trigger review. A flat section visible from the street might. We handle Philadelphia Historical Commission applications when they apply.

## Installation Quality Matters More Than the Brand

A common homeowner mistake is fixating on which membrane brand or product is "best." In practice, installation quality matters more than the membrane choice for the typical 20-to-30-year service life. A perfectly installed EPDM beats a sloppily installed TPO every time.

The installation details that actually drive service life:

- **Tear-off of the failed roof down to sound decking.** Skipping tear-off and installing over the existing roof is shorter-lived and harder to warranty.
- **Decking inspection and per-sheet replacement.** Every Philly rowhome roof we tear off needs at least 2 to 4 sheets of decking replaced. Skipping this leads to soft spots and early failure.
- **Insulation.** Adding rigid insulation board (polyiso or EPS) under the membrane improves energy performance and gives a smooth substrate.
- **Proper drainage design.** Either positive slope to internal drains or proper scupper drainage to the front gutter. Ponding water shortens any membrane's life.
- **Termination details.** Parapet wall flashing, drain bowls, vent pipe flashing, edge metal. Every termination is a potential leak point.
- **Permits and inspection.** Adilay Roofing pulls Philadelphia L&I permits on every flat roof job and the install is inspected at completion.

We document all of this in writing on every flat-roof project so you know exactly what's being installed and how.
