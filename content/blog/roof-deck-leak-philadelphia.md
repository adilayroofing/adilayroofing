# Leak Under a Roof Deck? How It's Fixed on a Philadelphia Rowhome

Rooftop decks are one of the best things about living in Fishtown, Northern Liberties, Graduate Hospital or South Philly. They're also one of the hardest roofs to keep dry. The deck sits right on top of the roof, so the roof underneath is out of sight for years — until a storm sends water through a top-floor ceiling.

Fixing a leak under a roof deck is a job a lot of roofers would rather avoid. It means taking the deck apart, redoing the roof, and putting the deck back. This guide explains why deck roofs fail, why the deck usually has to come off, and what a complete job looks like.

## Why Roofs Under Decks Fail

**Out of sight, out of mind.** You can't see the membrane under the boards, so small problems go unnoticed for years.

**Debris and moisture.** Leaves, dirt and grit fall between the boards and pile up on the roof, holding moisture against the membrane and blocking water from reaching the drain.

**Deck supports wear on the roof.** The sleepers or posts that hold the deck rest on the membrane. Over time they press, rub and sometimes puncture it — especially if they were set directly on the roof without protection.

**Blocked drains.** Drains and drain boxes under or beside the deck clog and get overlooked. Water ponds under the deck.

**Edges, walls and the pilot house.** The roof around the deck — parapet coping, the walls, the pilot house (the little structure at the top of the stairs) or roof hatch — has its own flashing that ages along with everything else.

## Signs the Roof Under Your Deck Is Leaking

- A **stain on the top-floor ceiling** under the deck, often after heavy rain rather than every rain
- **Water around the pilot house door** or at the top of the stairs
- **Soft or spongy spots** in the deck itself
- **Standing water** you can see between the boards days after rain
- An **intermittent leak** that seems to come and go — very common with deck roofs, since water travels a long way under the boards before finding a way in

## Why the Deck Usually Has to Come Apart

There's no good way to repair or replace a roof you can't reach. Lifting a few boards can be enough for a small leak right at the edge, but if the membrane under the deck is old, the leak keeps coming back, or water has been getting in for a while, the honest fix is:

1. Take the deck apart, carefully, so it can go back
2. Redo the roof underneath
3. Rebuild the deck

That's more work than either a roof or a deck alone, which is why some contractors offer only part of it: they'll take the deck off but won't put it back, or they'll patch around it and hope. Several of our customers have told us exactly that before they called us.

## What a Complete Job Looks Like

### 1. Take the deck down

Boards, railings and framing come apart in an order that lets them go back. Damaged pieces are noted so the homeowner knows what will be reused and what needs replacing.

### 2. Clear the roof and inspect the deck underneath

All the debris that has collected under the boards comes off. The old membrane comes off down to the wood, and the plywood is checked. Under decks, rot near drains and walls is common. Anything soft is replaced, and you're told first.

### 3. Fix the drainage

Drain boxes and downspouts get cleared or rebuilt so water leaves the roof freely, even with a deck on top. See our guide on [ponding water](/blog/ponding-water-flat-roof-philadelphia) for why this matters.

### 4. Install the new roof

We usually torch down new modified-bitumen membrane, run up the walls and parapets and around the pilot house. Where deck supports will rest, a smooth-surfaced layer or protective pads keep the posts from wearing through.

### 5. New flashing and coping

New aluminum flashing and [coping](/blog/roof-coping-capping-philadelphia-rowhome) around the edges and parapets, and new flashing around the pilot house or hatch.

### 6. Rebuild the deck

The deck goes back up, with supports set so they don't damage the new roof and with enough space underneath for water to drain and debris to be cleared.

### 7. Clean up

All old roofing and debris go to the dump.

## Keeping a Deck Roof Healthy

- **Clear debris** from under and between the boards once or twice a year if you can
- **Check the drain** after big storms and in the fall
- **Look at the top-floor ceiling** after heavy rain
- **Don't add heavy planters or hot tubs** without checking what the roof and deck can take
- **When you replace the roof, replace it under the deck too.** Roofing around a deck and leaving the old membrane underneath is the most common reason a "new roof" still leaks.

## The Bottom Line

A leak under a roof deck isn't a mystery — it's a roof that has gone unseen for too long. The fix is a complete job: deck down, roof redone with good drainage and edges, deck back up. It takes longer, but it's done once.
