# What to Check on Your New Roof Before You Pay the Final Bill

The crew is packing up, the dumpster is gone, and the roof looks great from the street. Now comes the final payment. For most homeowners this is the moment of least information: you can't easily see the roof, you don't know what to look for, and you're being asked to sign off.

This checklist is what we'd want our own family to check. It works for flat and shingle roofs, and none of it requires climbing onto the roof.

![A written roofing proposal being reviewed](/images/adilay-roofing-honest-pricing-proposal.jpg "Your signed scope is your checklist. Every numbered line on it should be done before final payment.")

## Start With the Written Scope

Pull out the signed estimate. A good one lists the work as numbered steps: what gets removed, how the plywood is handled, what membrane or shingles go on, the flashing and coping, gutters and downspouts, and debris removal. Go through it line by line. For each one, ask: was this done, and how do I know?

If your estimate was a single line with a price and the words "new roof," this step is harder — and it's worth reading [roofing estimate red flags](/blog/roofing-estimate-red-flags-philadelphia) before your next project.

## Ask for Photos

You shouldn't need to climb a ladder to verify a roof. A good contractor documents the job and shares:

- **Before** photos of the old roof
- **During** photos — especially the bare deck after tear-off, and any rotted plywood that was found and replaced
- **After** photos of the finished roof, edges, flashing and drains

If plywood replacement was billed, you should have seen it before it was done and you should have photos of it. That's the standard we work to.

## What to Check on a Flat Roof

From an upper window, the roof hatch, or photos:

- **The membrane is continuous**, with seams that look flat and fully bonded, no lifted edges or open laps
- **It runs up the walls and parapets** rather than stopping at the base of the wall
- **New aluminum flashing and coping** along every edge listed in the scope
- **The drain box and downspout** are new or cleared as promised, and the membrane is sealed into the drain
- **No standing water** a day or two after rain (a small amount at the edge can be normal; a pond in the middle is not — see [ponding water](/blog/ponding-water-flat-roof-philadelphia))
- **Old satellite dishes and mounts are gone** if their removal was in the scope
- **Siding at the wall joints is back on**, including on the neighbor's side if it was lifted

## What to Check on a Shingle Roof

From the ground, binoculars, or photos:

- **Shingle rows are straight** and evenly spaced
- **No lifted, curled or missing shingles**
- **Flashing at chimneys, walls and vents** is new metal, not just a bead of sealant
- **Drip edge and coping** along the edges
- **Vents and roof boots** are new where the scope said so
- **Ridge caps** are complete and lying flat

## What to Check From the Ground and Inside

- **Gutters and downspouts** are attached, pitched toward the downspout, and draining away from the house
- **No nails, shingles or debris** left in the yard, alley, gutters or on the porch roof
- **Landscaping and the neighbor's property** are as they were
- **Top-floor ceilings and the attic** show no new stains (and old stains are dry)
- **Skylights and attic hatches** are sealed

## Get Your Paperwork

Before final payment, make sure you have:

- **The signed scope** and any written change orders
- **The final invoice** matching the scope
- **The workmanship warranty** in writing, with its length in years
- **Manufacturer warranty** information, if it applies
- **Permit and inspection** sign-off, if a permit was required

If you ever sell the house, buyers will ask for these. Our guide to [roof warranty transfers](/blog/roof-warranty-transfer-philadelphia-rowhome) explains why.

## How to Raise a Problem

If something isn't right, say so before paying — calmly, specifically and in writing:

- **Point to the scope line** it relates to
- **Include a photo** if you can
- **Agree on what will be done and when**, in writing

Most issues at the end of a job are small: a downspout not reattached, debris in a gutter, a piece of coping to finish. A good contractor fixes them quickly. If a contractor pushes for full payment while scope items are clearly unfinished, that tells you something.

## The Bottom Line

You don't need to be a roofer to check a new roof. You need a written scope, photos, a few minutes at an upper window and in the attic, and your paperwork. If every line on the scope was done and you have proof, pay with confidence.
