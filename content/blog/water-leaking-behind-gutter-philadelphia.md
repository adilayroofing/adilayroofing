# Why Is Water Leaking Behind My Gutter? (Philadelphia Roofer's Guide)

If you're noticing a wet streak running down the wall behind your gutter, or a rotted strip of fascia between the gutter and the soffit, or water dripping from the underside of the eave during rain, you have a fixable problem that almost certainly does NOT require a new roof. ## What's Supposed to Happen at the Eave

Before getting into the four causes, here's how the eave is supposed to work.

Water hits the roof and runs down toward the eave. As it reaches the lower edge of the shingle field, it should be directed into a metal **drip edge** flashing that overhangs the fascia and tucks down into the back of the gutter. The drip edge directs the water away from the fascia (so it doesn't rot) and into the gutter (where it can drain away through the downspout).

In some installations there's also a **gutter apron** added at the gutter itself, which is a second piece of metal that wraps over the back of the gutter and against the fascia, providing extra protection at the gutter-to-fascia joint.

When this system is installed correctly, no water touches the back of the gutter, the fascia stays dry, and water flows cleanly to the downspout. When any part of it is wrong, water finds its way to the wrong place.

## Cause 1: Missing or Improperly Installed Drip Edge

This is the single most common cause on Philadelphia homes built before 2012, when drip edge wasn't yet required by code. We see it on Northeast Philly ranchers, South Philly rowhomes, and Bucks County Cape Cods from the 50s, 60s, and 70s.

### How You Know That's It

- Look up at the underside of the eave from below. If you can see the bottom edge of the shingles directly resting on the fascia, or hanging in air over the gutter with no metal flashing between them, the drip edge is missing.
- During rain, water drips from the back lower edge of the shingle (between the shingle and the fascia) rather than into the gutter.
- The fascia behind the gutter shows dark water staining or visible rot.

### The Fix

A drip edge can be retrofitted under the existing shingles without tearing off the roof. We carefully lift the lowest course of shingles, slide a new aluminum or galvanized steel drip edge into place under the shingle and over the fascia, nail it down, and re-seal the shingle.

**Typical cost on a Philadelphia rowhome front (15 to 25 linear feet):** $200 to $400.
**Typical cost on a larger twin or detached home (50 to 100 linear feet):** $500 to $1,200.

This is one of the highest-value fixes we do. A few hundred dollars now prevents thousands of dollars of fascia and soffit replacement later.

## Cause 2: Missing or Failed Gutter Apron

The gutter apron is a separate piece of flashing that wraps over the back of the gutter and against the fascia. Many older Philadelphia homes were installed with drip edge but no apron, which is sometimes adequate but more often allows wind-driven rain to push water between the gutter and the fascia.

### How You Know That's It

- The drip edge is present (visible metal flashing under the shingles).
- But you can see a visible gap between the back of the gutter and the fascia, or evidence of water staining or rust on the back side of the gutter.
- Leaks are worst during heavy wind-driven rain (nor'easter conditions in Philadelphia winter and spring).

### The Fix

A gutter apron retrofits over the existing drip edge and tucks behind the back of the gutter. Cost is similar to a drip edge retrofit:

**Typical cost on a rowhome front (15 to 25 linear feet):** $250 to $500.
**Typical cost on a larger home (50 to 100 linear feet):** $600 to $1,500.

If both drip edge and apron are missing (common on pre-1990 installs), we install both as a single retrofit job.

## Cause 3: Shingles Cut Too Short at the Eave

Some roofers install shingles flush with the edge of the drip edge rather than slightly overhanging it. This is wrong, but it happens, especially on cheap or rushed installs. Water then sheets off the shingle, hits the metal flashing at a sharp angle, and some of it follows the underside of the drip edge instead of dripping cleanly into the gutter.

### How You Know That's It

- The bottom edge of the shingles is exactly even with the edge of the drip edge metal, with no overhang.
- During rain, you can see water running back UNDER the drip edge rather than dripping forward into the gutter.
- This is more common on roofs installed in the last 10 to 15 years where the drip edge was added but the shingle install was sloppy.

### The Fix

Two options. The cleaner fix is to remove and re-install the bottom course of shingles with the proper 1/2 to 3/4 inch overhang past the drip edge. Cost: $400 to $1,200 depending on linear footage. The cheaper fix is to add an additional piece of flashing (called a "kick-out" or extended drip edge) that creates the proper water break. Cost: $250 to $700.

We diagnose which option is the right one based on the specific install we see on your roof.

## Cause 4: Ice Damming Lifting the Back of the Gutter

In winter, ice dams (covered in detail in our [ice dams in Philadelphia guide](/blog/ice-dams-philadelphia-prevention-removal)) form when warm attic air melts snow on the upper roof and the meltwater refreezes at the cold eave. As the ice dam grows, it can lift the entire back edge of the gutter away from the fascia, creating a permanent gap that water leaks through year-round, not just in winter.

### How You Know That's It

- The leak started during or after a winter with significant snow and ice.
- The gutter visibly tilts away from the fascia or has separated at the spike or hidden hanger.
- The eave is wet on the side closest to the front of the gutter, with water visible between the gutter back and the fascia.

### The Fix

This requires two steps: address the immediate gap (re-secure the gutter to the fascia with proper hangers, replace damaged spikes with screws, re-seal the back joint) AND address the ice dam cause (attic air sealing, insulation, ventilation, all covered in the ice dams guide).

**Cost for the immediate gutter repair:** $300 to $800 depending on the length of the affected run and whether fascia repair is also needed.
**Cost for the ice dam root-cause fix:** $1,500 to $5,000+ depending on attic access and insulation needs. This is a roof inspection question, not a gutter repair.

If the gutter has been ice-dam-damaged for multiple winters, the fascia underneath is almost always rotted and needs replacement during the gutter re-secure. We assess all of this in writing during the inspection.

## How to Diagnose Yours in 10 Minutes

If you want to figure out which cause you're dealing with before calling a contractor:

### Step 1: Wait for a Rain Event

Active rain makes everything obvious. If it's dry, look for evidence (stains, rot, ice damage) instead.

### Step 2: Look Up From Below the Eave

Stand on the ground or a step stool and look up at the underside of the eave from the corner of the house. You're looking for:
- The drip edge: is there visible metal flashing tucked under the shingles?
- The gutter back: is the gutter sitting tight against the fascia, or is there a visible gap?
- Active water flow: where exactly is the water coming from?

### Step 3: Look at the Fascia Behind the Gutter

If you can safely get a ladder up to the eave (don't if you can't), look at the fascia behind the gutter. You're looking for:
- Dark vertical staining (water has been running down the fascia for a while)
- Visible rot or soft spots in the wood
- Rust on the back of the gutter
- Visible gap between gutter and fascia

### Step 4: Document With Photos

A few clear photos from below the eave and from the top of the ladder are enough to give a contractor over the phone or by email a good first impression of what's happening.

### Step 5: Call for a Diagnosis

Most contractors (us included) will diagnose a gutter leak from a ladder for free as part of a standard inspection. If we tell you the fix is a $300 drip-edge retrofit, that's all you need. If we tell you it's a $2,000 ice-dam root-cause fix plus fascia repair, you have the full picture.

## What to Avoid Doing

A few well-intentioned moves often make the problem worse.

**Caulking the back of the gutter to the fascia.** Sometimes a homeowner runs a bead of silicone or polyurethane sealant along the back of the gutter where it meets the fascia. This works for one season. Then the gutter expands and contracts with temperature, the sealant cracks, water gets in, and now the water is trapped behind the sealant against the fascia where it rots much faster than it would have without the seal. Always address the drip edge or apron flashing instead.

**Adding gutter guards before fixing the leak.** Gutter guards add weight, can interfere with drainage, and don't address the four causes of water-behind-the-gutter leaks. Get the eave flashing right first; then add guards if you want them for debris control.

**Patching the fascia without fixing the flashing.** Rotted fascia happens because water has been getting to it. Replacing the fascia without fixing the drip edge or apron flashing just means the new fascia will rot too, in the same amount of time. Always do the flashing fix and the fascia repair together.

**Ignoring it because "the gutter still drains."** A leaking gutter-to-fascia joint slowly rots the fascia, soffit, and sometimes the rafter ends behind them. By the time the rot is visible from the ground, you're looking at $1,500 to $5,000 of repair work on top of the original $300 to $600 flashing retrofit you could have done at the start.
