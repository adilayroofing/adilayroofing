## The Five Biggest Red Flags

If any one of these is present, slow down. If two or more are present, walk away.

### Red Flag 1: No Pennsylvania HIC Registration Number on the Document

Pennsylvania law (Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act, 73 P.S. § 517.1) requires every contractor performing home improvement work over $500 to register with the Pennsylvania Attorney General and display the HIC registration number on every contract, advertisement, and estimate. There is no exception for roofing. The number looks like PA followed by 6 digits.

You can verify any HIC number for free at **attorneygeneral.gov/protect-consumers/hicsearch**. The public registry shows the registered business name, the registrant's name, the registration status (active, expired, suspended), and the expiration date.

If the estimate has no HIC number, ask for it. If the contractor refuses, or gives you a number that does not match their business name when you check the registry, or shows as expired or suspended, walk away. These are scammers' easiest tells.

### Red Flag 2: Large Cash Deposit Demand

Pennsylvania law caps deposits at one-third of the total contract price OR 10% plus the cost of special-order materials, whichever is higher. In practice, the legitimate range for a residential roof is 25% to 33% deposit.

If a contractor asks for 50%, 70%, or 100% upfront, or specifically asks for cash or a check made out to a personal name rather than a business, this is the single most common roofing-scam pattern in the Philadelphia market. Once the deposit is in their hands, they either start the job with cheap materials and walk away after the first phase, or they never come back at all. The cash makes the money untraceable.

Adilay Roofing's payment schedule is 33% deposit, 33% at substantial completion (after tear-off, decking repair, and underlayment are in), and 34% at final completion and walkthrough. We never accept cash for deposits over $500 and we never ask for checks to a personal name.

### Red Flag 3: No Written Estimate, or a Verbal Number Only

"I'll give you a great price, just give me the deposit and we'll start Monday." If you do not have a written, itemized estimate in your hand before signing, you have no contract. You have no recourse when the contractor doubles the price mid-project, switches to cheaper materials, or simply leaves.

The written estimate should be on company letterhead, identify the property address, identify both parties, name the scope of work, list the materials and quantities, list the labor, name the warranty terms, list the total price, and have a date and signature line. Anything less is a handshake, and a handshake is not enforceable when something goes wrong.

### Red Flag 4: High-Pressure Sales Tactics

The classic lines:

- "This price is only good today."
- "I have leftover materials from a job down the street, we can do yours cheap."
- "If you don't sign now, I have to drive back to the office and the price goes up."
- "I noticed your roof while I was working on your neighbor's house and wanted to give you a free inspection."

The "neighbor" line is the entry point for storm-chaser scams. After a hailstorm or nor'easter, out-of-town crews flood Philadelphia neighborhoods, especially older areas like Roxborough, Manayunk, Mount Airy, and the Northeast, knock on doors offering "free inspections," create damage that wasn't there before, and pressure homeowners into expensive contracts paid by insurance. Real, established Philadelphia roofers do not work this way. We schedule appointments, we provide identification, and we never pressure a same-day signature.

### Red Flag 5: Offering to Skip the Permit to Save You Money

Philadelphia L&I requires a building permit for residential roof replacement. Anyone offering to skip it to "save you money" is either not licensed, not insured, or both. We covered the full consequences in our [Philadelphia roofing permits guide](/blog/roof-permit-philadelphia), but the short version: skipping the permit risks $300+ fines per violation, voids your manufacturer warranty, may invalidate insurance claims, and surfaces during the title search when you sell. The permit fee is $40 to $200 on a typical residential roof. No reputable contractor pretends that is worth saving.

## What a Legitimate Roofing Estimate Has Line by Line

Use this as a checklist against any estimate sitting in front of you right now.

### Contractor Identification

- Company name, address, phone, email
- Pennsylvania HIC registration number (PAxxxxxx format)
- Philadelphia business license, if applicable to the work location
- Proof of general liability insurance (typically a Certificate of Insurance from the contractor's insurer)
- Proof of workers' compensation insurance for the crew

### Project Address and Scope

- Full property address
- Description of the work, by specific scope item:
  - Full tear-off vs. roof-over (if a roof-over is even legal under Philadelphia code, see our roof-over vs. tear-off guide for details)
  - Roof area to be replaced, in squares (1 square = 100 sq ft)
  - Decking inspection and per-sheet replacement pricing
  - Underlayment type
  - Ice-and-water shield coverage area
  - Drip edge installation
  - Flashing replacement (chimney, walls, skylights, valleys, penetrations)
  - Ventilation (ridge vent, soffit vents, gable vents)
  - Cleanup, magnetic nail sweep, dumpster removal

### Materials

- Brand and product line of the primary roofing material. "Architectural shingles" is not specific enough. "GAF Timberline HDZ in Charcoal" or "CertainTeed Landmark Pro in Weathered Wood" is what you want to see.
- Material warranty terms (typically 25 to 50 years on architectural shingles, depending on the line)
- Underlayment brand and type (synthetic underlayment is standard; felt paper is dated but still used)
- Ice-and-water shield brand
- Flashing material (aluminum is acceptable for general flashing, copper or 26-gauge painted steel preferred for chimney work)

### Permits and Code

- "Adilay Roofing will obtain the Philadelphia L&I building permit. Permit fee: $XX, included in total."
- Statement that the work will be performed to Philadelphia building code and inspected upon completion

### Warranty

- Manufacturer's warranty terms, in years, by component
- Contractor's workmanship warranty, in years, separately stated
- What voids each warranty
- How to file a warranty claim

### Pricing

- Itemized labor and materials, separately
- Total price, in dollars, not a range
- Payment schedule with deposit, progress payment, and final payment amounts
- Any allowances or contingencies (such as decking replacement priced per sheet), spelled out

### Signatures and Dates

- Customer signature line and date
- Contractor signature line and date
- Contract is binding upon both signatures

Anything less than this and you should ask for more before signing.

## Specific Patterns We See in Philadelphia

A few scams are particularly common in our market.

### The Insurance Inflation Job

A contractor approaches you after a storm, says "your roof has hail damage, your insurance will pay for the whole thing." They file a claim, the adjuster comes out, and the claim is approved at $12,000. The contractor invoices you for $12,000, you pay them, and they do $6,000 of work. The other $6,000 is profit. In some versions, the contractor asks you to sign over the insurance check directly to them. In Pennsylvania this practice (assignment of benefits) is legal but increasingly risky. Never sign over your check without seeing the actual scope-of-work and cost breakdown.

### The "While We're Up There" Upsell

The estimate comes in at a reasonable price. Halfway through the job, the contractor finds "rotted decking" or "structural damage" you can't easily verify and adds $4,000. This is sometimes legitimate (rotted decking does turn up on older homes), but it can also be invented. Protection: the original estimate should price decking replacement at a per-sheet rate ($75 to $150 per sheet) with the contractor required to show you photos of each replaced sheet before adding to the invoice.

### The Subcontractor Switch

The salesperson is a smooth professional with a clean truck and a Pennsylvania HIC number. They take your deposit. Three weeks later a different crew shows up in unmarked vehicles, doesn't speak English well, has no copy of the contract, and does work that doesn't match what you signed for. This is a contractor who closed the sale and sold the job to an unlicensed sub. Protection: the estimate should state in writing whether subcontractors will be used. If yes, they should be named, with their HIC numbers and insurance verified. Adilay Roofing never subcontracts. The crew on your roof is on our W-2s.

### The "We're Already in the Neighborhood" Discount

Anyone who knocks unsolicited claiming to have leftover materials from a nearby job and offering a discount is almost certainly a storm-chaser working out of a rental van. Real Philadelphia roofers schedule appointments, drive marked trucks, and don't carry leftover material around looking for buyers.
