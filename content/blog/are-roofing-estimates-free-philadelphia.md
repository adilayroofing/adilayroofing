## Why Roofers Give Free Estimates (It's Not Charity)

Understanding the economics kills the suspicion. Roofers give free estimates because:

1. **Accurate quotes require seeing the roof.** Roof size, pitch, layers, deck condition, flashing state, access — none of it is knowable from your description on the phone. The inspection *is* the quote. A roofer who quotes without looking is guessing, and guesses get revised upward mid-job.
2. **The estimate is how roofers compete.** You're going to get two or three quotes (you should). The company that shows up on time, explains what it found with photos, and hands you a clear written scope wins a lot of those comparisons. The estimate is the audition.
3. **The math works.** An estimator's hour is the customer-acquisition cost of a multi-thousand-dollar job. Spread across the jobs that close, free estimates are simply how the industry prices its sales process.

None of that requires tricks. Which is why the *behavior around* the free estimate — not the word "free" — is what tells you who you're dealing with.

## What a Real Free Estimate Includes

When our estimator leaves your Philadelphia rowhome or twin, you should have (or shortly receive) a document with:

- **Scope of work** — exactly what's being done, and where on the roof
- **Materials, by name** — the shingle line or membrane type and thickness, underlayment, flashing metal, ridge vent
- **Tear-off, disposal, and permits** — including the Philadelphia building permit when the job requires one ([our permit guide](/blog/roof-permit-philadelphia) explains when that is)
- **Both warranties** — manufacturer material warranty and the contractor's workmanship warranty, in years, in writing
- **The price and payment terms** — itemized enough that you can compare it against another company's quote line by line
- **License and insurance** — PA registration number on the paperwork (ours: PA184779), certificates on request

One line and a total is not an estimate. And a price that can't survive being written down is not a price.

## When "Free Estimate" Is a Trap: The Red Flags

The phrase "free roofing estimate" is also the front door of every high-pressure sales operation in the industry. The tells, from our [full red-flags guide](/blog/roofing-estimate-red-flags-philadelphia):

- **The 3-hour kitchen-table presentation.** A roof quote is not a timeshare pitch. If the "estimate" requires both spouses present and a laptop slideshow, you're in a sales funnel, not an inspection.
- **The price that expires today.** Real prices survive a week of thinking. "Sign now and it's $4,000 less" means the first number was fiction.
- **A firm price with no roof visit.** Satellite-measurement quotes are fine for a *ballpark*; anyone offering a contract-ready price without inspecting deck, flashing, and layers is planning change orders.
- **A big deposit before any work.** In Pennsylvania, be wary of contractors demanding large upfront payments at signing.
- **Door-knockers after storms.** Post-storm canvassers with "free inspections" are covered in our [storm checklist](/blog/storm-damage-roof-repair-philadelphia-checklist) — verify the PA license before anyone climbs your roof.

The pattern under every red flag: pressure replaces information. A legitimate free estimate gives you information and expects you to compare. **Expecting to be compared is the mark of a fair price.**
