# Philadelphia Winter Roof Damage: What to Check Before the Freeze

Here's the pattern we see every single year: the roofs that fail in February had problems in October — small ones. A lifted flashing corner. A gutter full of leaves. A membrane seam starting to open. Three months of freeze-thaw cycles, ice dams, and wet snow turn each of them into an interior leak, and the phone rings during the worst repair weather of the year.

Winter doesn't attack sound roofs; it audits weak ones. This guide is the audit you run first — what Philadelphia winters actually do to roofs, the fall checklist worth an hour of attention, and which findings mean "call someone before Thanksgiving."

## What Philadelphia Winter Actually Does to a Roof

**Freeze-thaw is the main event.** Philadelphia doesn't get Minnesota cold; it gets *cycling* — dozens of trips across 32°F each winter. Every crossing, water inside any crack or gap freezes and expands roughly 9%, prying the opening wider; the thaw sends water deeper; repeat. A hairline that shrugged off summer rain becomes a real gap by March. This is why winter damage concentrates at details: flashing joints, shingle cracks, membrane seams, mortar joints on parapets and chimneys.

**Ice dams work the eaves.** Heat escaping your attic melts the snow blanket from below; meltwater runs down to the cold overhang and refreezes into a ridge; the next melt pools behind the ridge and backs up *under* the shingles. The mechanism, prevention (ventilation and air-sealing, not just heat cables), and safe removal are covered fully in our [ice dam guide](/blog/ice-dams-philadelphia-prevention-removal).

**Wet snow loads flat roofs.** Rowhome flat roofs carry snow rather than shedding it. Dry powder is light; saturated snow and rain-on-snow events are the heavy case — slush approaches design loads fast, and it finds every low spot. A roof with existing ponding areas enters winter with its margin already spent (see the [ponding water guide](/blog/ponding-water-flat-roof-philadelphia) for why standing water was a defect in July, too).

**Condensation impersonates leaks.** Warm indoor air meeting cold roof structure condenses. Under-ventilated rowhome cavities drip on dry days and get misdiagnosed as roof leaks all winter — the [flat-roof winter leak guide](/blog/flat-roof-leaks-every-winter-philadelphia) untangles which is which.

## The Pre-Winter Checklist (October – Early November)

**From the ground, yourself:**

1. **Gutters and downspouts clear and attached.** Full gutters become ice trays that pry themselves off the fascia and feed ice dams. Watch where water goes in the next rain — overflow at the back edge is the [water-behind-the-gutter failure](/blog/water-leaking-behind-gutter-philadelphia) getting started.
2. **Shingle field scan with binoculars.** Lifted tabs, slipped or cracked shingles, missing pieces from fall wind — each is a freeze-thaw entry point.
3. **Flashing eyeball.** Chimney, vents, valleys: anything visibly lifted, rusted, or tar-smeared deserves professional eyes before ice tests it.
4. **Trees.** Limbs over the roof drop under snow load. Ice storms turn marginal limbs into roof punctures — trim before, not after.

**In the attic (15 minutes, flashlight):**

5. **Stains and daylight.** Water marks on sheathing, daylight where none belongs.
6. **Ventilation path open.** Soffit vents unblocked by insulation, air moving — choked intake is the ice-dam precondition, and it rots the roof from inside via winter condensation. (Rotted soffits *cause* blocked vents; the [soffit and fascia guide](/blog/soffit-fascia-repair-philadelphia) covers that loop.)
7. **Insulation coverage even.** Bare spots become warm roof patches become ice-dam melt zones.

**Professional, if anything above raised a flag — or the roof is 15+ years old:**

8. **Roof-level inspection**: seams and terminations on flat roofs, sealant condition, fastener back-out, drain function, deck softness. Under an hour for most homes; free with an estimate; and October findings get fixed in October weather.

## Flat-Roof Owners: Three Winter-Specific Items

Rowhome and flat-roof owners get a supplemental list:

- **Drains and scuppers clear — and kept clear.** A leaf-blocked drain under snow becomes a rooftop pond you won't see until it's inside. If your roof drains through a neighbor's scupper (rowhome roofs are a shared ecosystem), the courtesy check runs both ways.
- **Know your roof's low spots.** Where water ponded in summer is where slush will sit in January. If those areas are over prior repairs or soft deck, that's a pre-winter conversation, not a February surprise.
- **Have a snow plan for the big one.** Most winters need nothing. For the occasional 20-inch wet storm: professional removal beats a homeowner with a shovel on an icy membrane, every time. Warning signs that snow load is stressing the structure — new cracks, sticking doors, creaking — mean call, don't climb.

## If Winter Wins Anyway: Triage

Water coming in during a January thaw:

1. **Contain and drain** — buckets, plastic, poke bulging ceiling paint into a bucket.
2. **Photograph everything** for insurance and diagnosis.
3. **Stay off the roof.** Icy membranes and snow-hidden hazards make winter roofs professional territory.
4. **Call for stabilization** — [emergency response](/services/emergency-roof-repair) runs all winter: tarping, ice-dam relief, leak containment, then the permanent repair in the next workable window. The full sequence is in our [who-to-call leak guide](/blog/who-to-call-roof-leak-philadelphia).
