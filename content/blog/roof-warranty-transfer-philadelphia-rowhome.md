## The Two Different Warranties on Every Roof

When a roof gets installed, two separate warranties usually come with it. They cover different things, have different terms, and transfer differently. Confusing them is the single most common mistake we see homeowners make at closing.

### 1. The Manufacturer's Product Warranty

This is from the shingle or membrane manufacturer (GAF, CertainTeed, Owens Corning, Atlas, Malarkey, IKO, Carlisle, Firestone, etc.). It covers defects in the product itself: shingles that don't last as advertised, premature granule loss, manufacturer-side adhesion failures, membrane delamination.

- **Term:** typically 25 to 50 years on architectural shingles, with the "50-year" claim usually being a non-prorated period of 10 to 15 years followed by prorated coverage to year 50
- **What it covers:** the cost of replacement product (and sometimes labor) when a defect emerges
- **What it doesn't cover:** installation errors, storm damage, lack of maintenance, ice dam damage, pre-existing wear

### 2. The Contractor's Workmanship Warranty

This is from the contractor who installed the roof. It covers labor: installation errors that cause leaks, flashing failures, ventilation problems caused by improper install, etc.

- **Term:** typically 5 to 25 years depending on the contractor, with 10 years being the most common honest workmanship warranty in Philadelphia
- **What it covers:** repair or correction of any leak or failure caused by how the roof was installed
- **What it doesn't cover:** product defects, storm damage, normal wear

When you buy a house, the question is whether each of these warranties carries over to you. The answers are different.

## How Manufacturer Warranties Transfer

The major shingle manufacturers all allow exactly one transfer to a subsequent owner, but the terms differ.

### GAF (Most Common in Philadelphia)

GAF dominates the Philadelphia residential shingle market. Their warranty transfer rules:

- **One transfer allowed**, within 60 days of the sale
- **Submit the GAF Warranty Transfer form online or by mail**, with proof of installation, proof of sale, and the small transfer fee
- **The Standard limited warranty (lifetime to original owner)** transfers but the coverage typically steps down to a defined period (often 20 years) for the second owner
- **GAF System Plus** (a registered system requiring 3+ GAF accessories) transfers with reduced coverage
- **GAF Golden Pledge** (full system + GAF-certified contractor) includes transferable workmanship coverage in some cases. Check the original certificate.

### CertainTeed

CertainTeed's transfer rules are similar to GAF's:

- **One transfer allowed**, with a window typically defined as 60 days from sale
- **Transfer fee** typically $50 to $100
- **SureStart Plus** warranties include some transferable labor coverage for a defined period
- **Standard limited warranty** transfers with the same step-down pattern as GAF

### Owens Corning

- **One transfer allowed**, within 60 days
- **TruDefinition Duration warranties** transfer with reduced terms
- **Preferred Contractor warranties** include transferable workmanship coverage in certain product tiers

### Other Brands

Atlas, Malarkey, IKO, and others have generally similar one-transfer policies. Read the specific warranty document.

### Carlisle, Firestone, GenFlex (Flat Roof Membranes)

For EPDM, TPO, and PVC membranes on rowhome flat roofs, manufacturer warranties typically run 10 to 30 years and transfer differently. Many flat-roof system warranties are tied to the building, not the owner, and transfer automatically with the sale. Others require explicit transfer paperwork. Check the specific warranty document and contact the manufacturer or installing contractor.

## How Workmanship Warranties Transfer (Usually They Don't)

The contractor's workmanship warranty is where things get specific to the original contract. Most workmanship warranties name the original homeowner and end at the sale of the house. A few do transfer:

- **If the original contract explicitly says "transferable to subsequent owners"**, it transfers. Some contractors include this; many don't.
- **If the warranty is part of a manufacturer's system warranty** like GAF Golden Pledge or CertainTeed SureStart Plus, the workmanship portion may transfer with the manufacturer warranty.
- **If the original contractor is willing to honor a transfer voluntarily**, some will, particularly if the buyer notifies them promptly and shows the original contract.

Adilay Roofing's standard workmanship warranty is 10 years, transferable once with no fee, provided the buyer notifies us within 60 days of closing and has the original contract and inspection documentation.

If the original contractor is out of business (a common scenario for Philadelphia homes where the install was 8 to 15 years ago and the contractor was a small operation), the workmanship warranty is essentially dead. There is no one to enforce it against.

## What to Ask the Seller (Five Questions That Cost Nothing)

Before you sign, ask the seller's agent to put these answers in writing in the seller's disclosure addendum.

### Question 1: Who Installed the Roof, When, and Are They Still in Business?

The name and HIC registration number of the original contractor, the installation date, and whether the contractor is still operating. You can verify HIC registration at attorneygeneral.gov/protect-consumers/hicsearch. If the contractor is out of business, the workmanship warranty is effectively dead and the only thing left is the manufacturer warranty.

### Question 2: What Was the Warranty Tier?

Manufacturer warranties come in several tiers with very different coverage. GAF has Standard, System Plus, Silver Pledge, and Golden Pledge. CertainTeed has Standard, SureStart, and SureStart Plus. The seller should be able to show you the warranty certificate. If they can't, you can sometimes pull it from the manufacturer using the address and installer information.

### Question 3: Is the Original Installation Paperwork Available?

The original contract, installation invoice, warranty registration confirmation, and any manufacturer warranty certificate should transfer to you at closing. Without these documents, transferring the warranty is much harder. If the seller does not have them, ask if the original contractor can provide copies (often they can, for active customers).

### Question 4: Was the Install Permitted Through Philadelphia L&I?

An unpermitted installation can void the manufacturer warranty regardless of transfer status. Most major shingle manufacturers require installation in compliance with local code, which means with a permit. You can check Philadelphia L&I permit records online at the Atlas Philadelphia GIS portal or through the eCLIPSE portal. If no roof permit appears on the property record for the year of installation, ask the seller.

### Question 5: Has Anyone Repaired the Roof Since Installation, and If So, Who?

Repair work by a contractor other than the original installer can sometimes void the original workmanship warranty (the original contractor can argue the failure was caused by the later repair). It also creates a documentation chain that the new owner needs to manage. Ask for any repair invoices.

## How to Actually Transfer the Warranty After Closing

Once you close, you have 60 days (or whatever window the specific warranty defines) to file the transfer. The general process:

1. **Locate the original installation paperwork** the seller provided at closing
2. **Find the manufacturer warranty registration** (sometimes a paper certificate, sometimes an online registration number)
3. **Visit the manufacturer's transfer page** (gaf.com/transfer, certainteed.com/warranty, owenscorning.com/warranty, etc.)
4. **Complete the transfer form** with your name, the previous owner's name, the closing date, the property address, and proof of sale (a copy of the closing disclosure works)
5. **Pay the transfer fee** if any (typically $50 to $100)
6. **Receive confirmation** of the transferred warranty, usually by email within 7 to 14 days
7. **Notify the original contractor** in writing of the sale and your contact information, if the workmanship warranty is transferable

Keep all documentation. If a claim ever arises, you will need to produce the transfer confirmation along with the original installation paperwork.

## The Pre-Purchase Roof Inspection: $300 to $500 That Protects $20,000

A general home inspector will glance at the roof from the ground or with a ladder at the eaves. They are not roofers. They miss:

- Lifted or improperly-installed flashing
- Failed seam adhesion on flat roof sections
- Decking sponginess that signals water intrusion below the surface
- Manufacturer registration status of the visible shingles
- Whether the install matches what the warranty documents claim

A pre-purchase roof inspection by a licensed Philadelphia roofer (we do these for $350 across Philadelphia and the surrounding counties) includes:

- Walk of the entire roof surface
- Inspection of every flashing, vent, chimney, and penetration
- Attic check for moisture, daylight, mold, and ventilation
- Photo documentation of all findings
- Written assessment of remaining service life
- Verification of warranty transferability based on what we can see
- Recommendations on what to address before closing vs. after

The cost is recovered many times over the first time it surfaces a real issue. Common findings on Philadelphia homes in their first 10 years of roof life:

- Missing ice-and-water shield (visible at eaves from the attic)
- Flashing sealed with caulk instead of properly stepped and counter-flashed
- Bath fans venting into the attic instead of through the roof
- Ridge vent installed but not cut through (visible from the attic)
- "30-year shingles" that are actually 25-year 3-tab on closer inspection

Any one of these is grounds to negotiate either a repair credit at closing or a price adjustment.
