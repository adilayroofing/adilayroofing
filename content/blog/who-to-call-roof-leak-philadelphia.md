## Why a Roofer — Not a Handyman, GC, or "Roof Guy"

The hard part of leak repair isn't the patch. It's the diagnosis.

Water is a liar. It enters at a flashing joint near the chimney, runs eight feet down a rafter, drips onto the ceiling, and surfaces as a stain nowhere near the entry point. On Philadelphia's flat rowhome roofs it's worse — water travels between membrane layers and shows up two rooms away, sometimes in your *neighbor's* rowhome.

- **A handyman** patches where the stain is. The visible symptom gets caulk; the entry point keeps leaking. You pay twice — once now, once when it comes back with a bigger water bill in damage.
- **A general contractor** will take the job, then call a roofing sub and add a management layer to the price.
- **A licensed roofer** traces the water path to its source — hose testing when needed, moisture readings, checking the usual suspects in order (flashing, penetrations, seams, valleys) — then fixes the entry point. That's the difference between [professional roof leak repair](/services/roof-leak-repair) and leak whack-a-mole.

The license matters for a boring reason too: insured contractors carry the liability when someone's on your roof. In PA, verify any contractor's registration number before they climb — ours is PA184779.

## What to Do in the Next 30 Minutes (Before Anyone Arrives)

1. **Contain**: bucket under the drip, plastic or towels over floors and furniture, electronics out of the room.
2. **Drain trapped water**: bulging ceiling paint is a water balloon — poke a small screwdriver hole and drain it into the bucket. A controlled drip beats a collapsed ceiling panel.
3. **Photograph everything**: the drip, the stain, the room, a timestamp. Useful for insurance; useful for the roofer.
4. **Kill power** to any circuit with water near its fixtures.
5. **Stay off the roof.** Wet shingles and membranes send homeowners to the ER every storm season. The tarp is a crew job.
