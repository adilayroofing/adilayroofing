# Bay Window Roof Leaks on Philadelphia Homes: Causes and Fixes

Bay windows give Philadelphia rowhomes and twins a lot of their character — that bump-out on the front of the house, usually on the second floor, often sitting right over the porch. Each one has its own small roof. And that small roof is one of the most common leak points on the front of the house.

When water shows up around a bay window, most people suspect the window. Often it's the roof above it. This guide covers how bay window roofs fail and what a lasting repair looks like.

## How Bay Window Roofs Fail

**The wall joint.** The bay roof meets the front wall along its top edge. That joint needs flashing that runs up behind the siding or into the brick. When it's missing, failed or replaced with roofing cement, water running down the wall goes behind the bay roof and into the wall.

**Old or patched roofing.** Many bays still have original slate, copper or old shingles, patched many times. Broken slate and worn shingles let water through.

**The edges.** Bay roofs are small with a lot of edges. Loose flashing or missing coping along those edges lets water reach the wood.

**Rotted wood.** Because water sits in such a small area, the plywood and trim under a leaking bay roof rot fast.

**Shared water with the porch.** A bay over a porch sheds water onto the porch roof. If the joint between them isn't lapped correctly, both leak.

## Signs It's the Bay Roof

- Stains on the **ceiling inside the bay** or on the wall just above it
- Water around the **top of the bay window frames**, not the bottom
- **Peeling paint or rot** on the trim just under the bay roof edge
- **Broken or slipped slate**, or curled shingles, visible from the street
- A stain on the **porch ceiling** right under the bay

## What a Lasting Repair Includes

These come from how we write front-of-house scopes:

1. **Remove the old roofing** on the bay down to the wood.
2. **Lift the siding or open the flashing at the wall** so new roofing can run up behind it.
3. **Replace damaged plywood**, telling you first.
4. **Install new roofing**: torch-down membrane on a nearly flat bay, or shingles over synthetic underlayment on a sloped one. Some old slate bays are repaired with matching slate; others are replaced with shingles or new plywood and siding, depending on the house.
5. **New aluminum flashing and coping** around the bay roof edges, set in flashing cement.
6. **Reset siding** at the wall, and **coping around the window trim** if the wood is failing.
7. **Clean up** and haul away all debris.

## Do It With the Porch Roof When You Can

On a lot of Philadelphia fronts, the bay roof, the porch roof below it and the siding around them were all built together and are all aging together. We often write them as a single scope — porch roof and bay window roof — because one setup is cheaper than two, and every joint between them can be lapped correctly.
